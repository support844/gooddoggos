H1: Puppy Foundations in San Jacinto

If you’ve just brought home a new puppy and your rugs are taking a beating, you’re in the "land shark" phase. Those sharp teeth are no joke, and neither is the frustration of trying to get a 10-week-old ball of energy to pay attention in the San Jacinto heat. Letting them "be puppies" without structure is how you end up with a 70-pound adult dog that drags you toward every person at Hiram Deville Park. You want to get the foundation right before the bad habits start.

Building Success from Day One
At Good Doggos, I don’t believe puppies are too young for clear communication. For 8 years, I’ve shown Riverside County owners that "balanced" training for puppies is about setting the stage for neutrality. I use heavy rewards (food, toys, praise) to build engagement, but I also introduce boundaries early. If you only ever teach them "yes," they’ll have a meltdown the first time life tells them they can't do something.

I remember a puppy "land shark" that was biting children in a local home. By establishing boundaries early—using age-appropriate structure and a firm, fair "no"—we stopped the behavior before it became an ingrained habit. I even used my stable pack, Chico and Rocky, as puppy-safe role models to show the pup how a balanced dog behaves. Chico, my "Icebreaker," is perfect for teaching pups that they don't have to be frantic to get attention.

We don’t just stay in your living room. I’ll show you how to expose your pup to the sounds of the Ramona Expressway and Valley-Wide Recreation without overwhelming them. By the time they hit adolescence, your dog won't be looking for trouble; they’ll be looking to you for direction.
