These possessive behaviors are symptoms of an entitlement to resources that "trading" for treats won't fix. Because we are professionals, we do not guess or give out generic advice. Reclaiming Your Space and Your Gear
At Good Doggos, I don't believe you should live in fear of your dog's "treasures." For 8 years, I’ve specialized in dismantling guarding behavior using a firm, balanced methodology. I use the "Parenting Analogy"—we wouldn't let a child dictate who can enter the kitchen, and we shouldn't enable a dog's entitlement over gear.

I remember a case in a San Jacinto ranch house where a dog would guard an entire laundry basket. We didn't "bribe" the dog out; we established that the basket (and the space around it) belonged to the leader. Using clear spatial pressure and calibrated leash corrections to interrupt the "guarding posture"—the freezing and the side-eye—we restored the household hierarchy. Once the dog realized their "management" role was over, they could finally relax.
