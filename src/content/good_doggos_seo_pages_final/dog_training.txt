Real-World Reliability Through Balance
At Good Doggos, I don't believe in "one size fits all" or fluff. I’ve spent 8 years in Riverside County refining a balanced approach that actually works when the distractions are high. I use plenty of praise and rewards to motivate your dog, but I also believe in the "Parenting Analogy"—we wouldn't let a child make major decisions until they are competent, and we shouldn't enable a dog's bad choices.

This means using "Balanced Clarity": high-value rewards for the right choices and calibrated leash corrections to tell your dog "no" when they ignore a command. If you don't have a way to interrupt bad choices, you’re just making suggestions, not training.
