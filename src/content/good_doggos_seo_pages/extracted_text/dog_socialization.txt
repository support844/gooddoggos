If you think "socialization" means taking your dog to the nearest dog park in San Jacinto and letting them "sort it out" with twenty strangers, you are setting your dog up for a disaster. True socialization isn't about your dog being best friends with every golden retriever they meet at Hiram Deville Park. It’s about neutrality. It’s about your dog being able to walk past a screaming toddler, a barking reactive dog, or a cyclist on State Street without losing their cool. If your dog is currently lunging, whining, or hiding behind your legs the moment you leave your Riverside County driveway, they aren't "socialized"—they’re overwhelmed.
The Art of Controlled Exposure
At Good Doggos, we don't believe in "throwing them in the deep end." For 8 years, we’ve specialized in reclaiming the narrative on socialization for the dogs of the Inland Empire. Our balanced approach focuses on building confidence through structured, calm exposure. We use high-value rewards and enthusiastic praise to reward your dog for choosing to look at you instead of the "scary" or "exciting" thing in the environment. However, we also believe in the power of a clear boundary. We use calibrated leash work to provide a physical "interrupt" if your dog decides to fixate or escalate. If you don't teach your dog how to be neutral, you're just a passenger on a high-speed emotional rollercoaster.
