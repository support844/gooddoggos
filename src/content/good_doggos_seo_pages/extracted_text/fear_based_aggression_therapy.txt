If your dog’s first instinct when meeting a stranger or another dog is to show teeth, lung, or snap, you aren't dealing with a "mean" dog—you’re dealing with a terrified one. In the dog world, the best defense is a good offense. It’s a gut-wrenching feeling when a simple walk near Valley-Wide Regional Park turns into a scene because your dog feels cornered by the world. You’ve probably tried to soothe them or feed them treats to show them "everything is okay," only to realize that when the adrenaline hits in the San Jacinto heat, your dog can’t even see the treat in front of their face. They are in survival mode, and "positive-only" distraction isn't a safety net when a bite is on the line.
Moving from Survival to Security
At Good Doggos, we specialize in the delicate balance of fear-based aggression. For 8 years, we’ve been the "last resort" for Riverside County owners who have been told their dog is a liability. Our balanced therapy focuses on building a foundation of trust through clear communication. We use high-value rewards and heavy praise to create new, positive associations with the things that scare them. However, a fearful dog also needs to know that you are in control of the situation so they don't have to be. We use calibrated leash corrections to interrupt the aggressive escalation, providing a firm boundary that prevents the "explosion" and allows the dog’s brain to settle down and actually process our training.
